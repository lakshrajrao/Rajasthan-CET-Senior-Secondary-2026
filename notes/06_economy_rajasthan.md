### B. STORY-BASED EXPLANATION — a big state with a dry backbone

Rajasthan is India's largest state by area, and its economy is built on what its land gives it. The western ~60% is the Thar desert, so farming there depends on scarce rain and groundwater; the south-east has the Chambal's water and black soil; the Aravalli divides the two worlds. Because water is the scarcest input, the state's economy has two clear faces: **where water and minerals exist, industry and cash crops grow** (cement in Chittorgarh, zinc at Zawar, oil in Barmer, solar in the desert sun); **where they do not, the economy leans on rain-fed farming, livestock and handicrafts**. The Budget 2026-27 puts the size of this economy at a GSDP of about **₹21.52 lakh crore** (2025-26 projection), with per capita income of about **₹2,02,349** — and its stated direction is "Viksit Rajasthan @2047".

### C. WHY DOES IT HAPPEN? — why the economy looks the way it does

- **Why does agriculture dominate the issues list?** Because most of the state's cropped area is rain-fed: rainfall is low and uncertain, and irrigation comes mostly from wells and tubewells (canals second). When rain fails, the farm economy — and the rural economy with it — feels it first. This is the direct link to the syllabus topics of drought and famine (Chapter 6).
- **Why is solar a strength in a desert state?** The same harsh sun and empty land that make farming hard make solar power cheap to build: Rajasthan is No.1 in India in solar energy, with the Bhadla solar park (Jodhpur) as its flagship. (The exact capacity figures in the bank are unverified — see Chapter 2.)
- **Why do minerals matter so much here?** The Aravalli is one of the world's oldest mountain ranges and is mineral-rich: zinc-lead at Zawar (India's largest), marble, limestone, gypsum — plus petroleum in Barmer and natural gas in Jaisalmer. Minerals are the reason the industrial map of Rajasthan is not evenly spread.
- **Why is per capita income only a rough guide?** It is an average (GSDP ÷ population). It hides the gap between the industrial pockets and the dry districts. The exam asks the figure; the understanding is that the figure is an average, not a description of every household.

### D. HOW DOES IT WORK? — how to answer a "features & issues" question

1. **Start from the land:** desert west, Aravalli middle, plain/plateau east — every economic feature follows from this.
2. **Attach one fact to each region:** west → solar + oil + salt; centre → marble + cement; south-east → Chambal water + crops + dams.
3. **Name the constraint:** water (rain + groundwater) is the binding constraint on farming; distance and desert are the constraint on transport and industry.
4. **End with the direction:** the Budget's stated vision is Viksit Rajasthan @2047 — a $4.3 trillion economy by 2047.

### B. STORY-BASED EXPLANATION — what the land gives, the factory takes

Take a farmer's field in Sri Ganganagar: cotton grows there because canal water reaches it. That cotton goes to the textile mills of **Bhilwara** — called the "Manchester of Rajasthan" — and Pali. Now take a truckload of limestone from the Aravalli to **Chittorgarh**, where India's leading cement industry stands. Take zinc ore from **Zawar** to the smelter at **Chanderiya** (Hindustan Zinc). Take crude oil from **Barmer's** Mangala, Bhagyam and Aishwariya fields, and natural gas from **Jaisalmer**. And in the fields themselves: **bajra and sarso are No.1 in India**, spices (jeera, dhaniya, isabgol, methi) lead the country, and the desert sun makes Rajasthan **No.1 in solar energy** with the Bhadla mega solar park in Jodhpur. Each sector is a link in one chain: land → raw material → industry → market.

### C. WHY DOES IT HAPPEN? — why each sector sits where it sits

- **Why is cement in Chittorgarh?** Cement needs limestone, and the Aravalli around Chittorgarh supplies it in bulk. Industry follows the raw material — it is cheaper to build the factory near the quarry than to carry stone across the state.
- **Why is Bhilwara the "Manchester of Rajasthan"?** Textile towns grow where cotton, labour and trade routes meet. Bhilwara's mills process the cotton grown in the canal-irrigated north (Sri Ganganagar, Hanumangarh) — the same logic that made Manchester the textile town of England (a comparison of function, not of size).
- **Why is Rajasthan No.1 in bajra and sarso?** Both are dry-land crops: bajra needs little water and sarso grows in the cool rabi season on limited moisture. They suit exactly the climate that makes rice and wheat hard here — the crop matches the rainfall.
- **Why is solar No.1 here?** High solar radiation plus vast empty desert land plus low competing land use. The constraint is not sun but transmission and storage — the exam does not ask that, but it explains why the parks are in Jodhpur, Bikaner and Jaisalmer (Budget 2026-27: new solar parks in Bikaner & Jaisalmer, 4,830 MW, joint venture).
- **Why is irrigation mostly from wells?** Because canal water reaches only part of the state (Indira Gandhi Canal in the north-west; Chambal in the south-east). The rest of farming depends on groundwater — which is why water, not land, is the binding constraint (Chapter 1).

### D. HOW DOES IT WORK? — how to answer any sector question

1. **Ask "what is the raw material, and where is it?"** — the answer is usually the district.
2. **Ask "what is made from it, and where?"** — the factory town is usually near the raw material.
3. **For crops, ask "kharif ya rabi, aur kitna pani?"** — bajra (kharif, dry), sarso (rabi, low water), kapas (irrigated north), ganna (water-heavy, Sawai Madhopur/Bundi).
4. **For "No.1" claims, attach the exact item** — bajra No.1, sarso No.1, solar No.1, zinc-lead (Zawar) India's largest. Never swap the items.

### B. STORY-BASED EXPLANATION — a budget is a family's annual plan, at state scale

Think of the state budget the way you would think of a household's yearly plan: first you count what the family earns (revenue), then you decide what to spend on food and daily needs (revenue expenditure) and what to spend on things that last — a house, a shop, a road (capital expenditure). Rajasthan's Budget 2026-27, presented on **11 February 2026** by Finance Minister **Diya Kumari**, is that plan at state scale: a total size of **₹6,10,956 crore**, of which **₹53,978 crore** is capital expenditure — money that builds assets. The state spends more than it earns, so there is a **fiscal deficit of about 3.69% of GSDP (₹79,492 crore)** and a **revenue deficit of ₹24,313 crore**. The whole economy behind the budget — the GSDP — is projected at **~₹21.52 lakh crore** for 2025-26, and the average income per person at **~₹2,02,349**. The budget's stated direction is **Viksit Rajasthan @2047**: a developed Rajasthan by 2047, with a $4.3 trillion economy.

### C. WHY DOES IT HAPPEN? — why the budget has these parts

- **Why is capital expenditure singled out?** Because it creates assets that keep paying: roads (42,000 km announced), solar parks, water missions. Revenue expenditure is consumed in the year; capital expenditure builds the next decade. The exam asks the capital figure precisely because it signals the state's investment direction.
- **Why does a deficit exist at all?** Because the state spends more than its revenue to build infrastructure and run welfare schemes. A fiscal deficit is normal; the question is its size relative to GSDP (~3.69%) — the ratio, not the rupee figure alone, is what economists compare.
- **Why is GSDP bigger than the budget?** GSDP (~₹21.52 lakh crore) is everything produced in the state by everyone — farmers, factories, shops, services. The budget (₹6,10,956 crore) is only the *government's* spending. Confusing the two is the commonest trap in this chapter.
- **Why is PPP asked (PYQ 2024-P3 Q18)?** Because "Purchasing Power Parity" is a standard income concept: it compares economies by what money actually buys in each country, not by the exchange rate. It belongs with state/national income concepts, not with the budget figures.
- **Why is the RSTA announcement in the budget?** The Rajasthan State Testing Agency (RSTA) — on the lines of the NTA — was announced in Budget 2026-27. New institutions are announced in budgets because a budget is also a policy statement, not just a money statement.

### D. HOW DOES IT WORK? — how to read any budget question

1. **Ask "which figure?"** — total size (₹6,10,956 cr) vs capital expenditure (₹53,978 cr) vs deficit (fiscal ₹79,492 cr / revenue ₹24,313 cr) vs GSDP (~₹21.52 lakh cr). Four different numbers; the question always names one.
2. **Ask "who presented it, and when?"** — Diya Kumari, 11 Feb 2026. (The presenter changes; the date fixes the budget.)
3. **Ask "concept ya figure?"** — PPP, fiscal deficit, revenue deficit, GSDP, per capita income are *concepts* (stable). Their *figures* change every year (dated).
4. **Ask "announcement ya amount?"** — RSTA, Carbon Credit Pilot, solar parks (Bikaner & Jaisalmer, 4,830 MW, JV), 42,000 km roads, CM Jal Jeevan Mission ₹6,800 cr, Thar Cultural Circuit ₹5,000 cr (⚠️ verify before exam, §H6) are *announcements*; the question may ask the scheme or the amount.

### B. STORY-BASED EXPLANATION — the economy you can hold in your hand

Not all of Rajasthan's economy is in big factories. In Khandela (Sikar), artisans stitch **gota-patti** — ribbons of gold and silver thread — onto cloth for weddings and festivals. In Jaipur, hands shape **blue pottery** (quartz, not clay — see Art & Culture notes), cut **gems and jewellery**, and carve marble. These are **handicrafts**: products made by hand, carrying a place's name and a community's skill, sold in bazaars and exported. Beside them stand the **small enterprises** — the district-level textile units of Bhilwara and Pali, leather and wood work, tie-dye (Bandhej) — small units that employ local people without the scale of a big factory. The syllabus puts handicrafts and small enterprises together because both are the same economic idea: **local skill + local material = local employment**, without waiting for a mega-project.

### C. WHY DOES IT HAPPEN? — why handicrafts survive in a modern economy

- **Why does gota-patti come from Khandela (Sikar)?** Handicrafts cluster where a community has practised the skill for generations and where the raw material and the market are close. The exam asks the place because the place *is* the identity of the craft — "Gota industry, Khandela, Sikar" was a 2024 PYQ (2024-P4 Q23) for exactly this reason.
- **Why do small enterprises matter for employment?** Because they need little capital and use local labour. A big cement plant employs few people per crore invested; a hundred small textile and craft units employ many. That is why the syllabus links small enterprises to the unemployment topic (Chapter 5).
- **Why is the GI tag relevant?** A Geographical Indication tag legally ties a product to its place (e.g. Kota Doria, Blue Pottery — see Art & Culture notes). It protects the artisan from imitation and raises the price. The tag is the *economic* side of a *cultural* product — which is why this chapter and the Art & Culture head overlap.

### D. HOW DOES IT WORK? — how to answer a handicraft question

1. **Ask "craft ↔ place"** — gota-patti ↔ Khandela (Sikar); blue pottery ↔ Jaipur; gems & jewellery ↔ Jaipur. The question is almost always a matching question.
2. **Ask "handicraft ya industry?"** — hand-made, traditional, community skill = handicraft. Machine/mills, larger scale = industry (Chapter 2).
3. **Ask "GI tag hai ya nahi?"** — if the craft has a GI tag, the exam may ask the place through the tag.
4. **Do not invent a craft for a district.** If the notes do not name a craft for a place, say so — do not guess.

### B. STORY-BASED EXPLANATION — the government as a safety net

A welfare scheme is the government stepping in where the market does not reach: a poor family that cannot pay a hospital bill, a girl who might drop out of school, a farmer whose crop fails, a young person with no job. Rajasthan runs its own schemes and implements the Centre's. On health: **Mukhyamantri Ayushman Arogya Yojana** (earlier called Chiranjeevi, ₹25 lakh — the current name and amount must be checked before the exam) and the Centre's **Ayushman Bharat (PM-JAY, 2018)** — ₹5 lakh per family. On girls' education: **Gargi Puraskar** and **Balika Protsahan Puraskar**. On women's livelihood: **Lakhpati Didi** and **Rajeevika** (self-help groups). On youth employment: **Mukhyamantri Yuva Swarojgar Yojana**. On farmers: **PM-KISAN** (₹6,000/year since 2019), **PM Fasal Bima** (2016), **PM-KUSUM** (solar pumps). On housing and water: **PM Awas Yojana** (2015) and **Jal Jeevan Mission** (2019, Har Ghar Jal). Each scheme is one row in a table: who benefits, how much, since when — and the exam asks exactly those three things.

### C. WHY DOES IT HAPPEN? — why schemes are the exam's favourite topic

- **Why are schemes asked so often?** Because a scheme is a testable fact with three fixed parts (beneficiary, benefit, year). The bank has 7 of its 18 questions on schemes, and the mock papers add 9 more — schemes are the highest-frequency topic in this subject.
- **Why do scheme names change?** Governments rename schemes (Chiranjeevi → Ayushman Arogya). The exam can ask the *current* name or the *old* one — which is why the old name is kept in the table with a "check current" flag instead of being deleted.
- **Why are six figures in this chapter flagged unverified?** Because they come from Budget speeches and CM speeches reported in November 2026 (TOI, IANS) and the audit could not check them against a primary source (register §H3–H7). The figures are *plausible* and the bank tests them — but a student must know they are "as per the bank" until verified, not settled fact.
- **Why is Lakhpati Didi in both state and centre rows?** Because it is a national mission (3 crore Lakhpati Didis target) *implemented* by the state, and the state Budget 2026-27 raised its loan limit. The same scheme can be asked from either side.

### D. HOW DOES IT WORK? — how to answer any scheme question

1. **Ask "kis ke liye?"** (beneficiary) — girl / woman / farmer / youth / artisan / family.
2. **Ask "kitna?"** (benefit) — the amount, and whether it is per year, per family, or a loan limit.
3. **Ask "kab se?"** (since when) — the launch year (PM-KISAN 2019, Ayushman Bharat 2018, PM Awas 2015, Fasal Bima 2016, Jal Jeevan 2019, Vishwakarma 2023, MGNREGA 2005).
4. **Ask "rajya ya kendra?"** — the same benefit can exist in both (Ayushman Arogya = state; Ayushman Bharat = centre; Lakhpati Didi = both).
5. **If the amount carries a ⚠️ flag, answer it as the bank states it** — and verify before the exam.

### B. STORY-BASED EXPLANATION — factories do not appear by themselves

A factory needs land, power, water, a road and permission — and someone has to provide all five before the first brick is laid. That "someone" is a development institution. In Rajasthan the main one is **RIICO — the Rajasthan State Industrial Development and Investment Corporation** — which develops industrial areas and gives industry the land and facilities it needs. The state's **Industrial Policy** sets the rules for investment. And where the state itself runs a business, that business is a **public sector undertaking (PSU)**: the oldest of them is the **Rajasthan State Ganganagar Sugar Mills (RSGSM)**. Two PYQs in 2024 asked about this world — one about the oldest PSU (2024-P4 Q26) and one whose subtopic is "DIGGI construction" (2024-P4 Q25), for which the repo has no further detail. The lesson of both PYQs is the same: the exam asks institutions by name, and the name is the answer.

### C. WHY DOES IT HAPPEN? — why institutions exist

- **Why does industry need RIICO?** Because a single factory cannot buy a whole industrial area, build its roads and arrange its power. RIICO does that once, for many factories — it is cheaper for the state to build the shared infrastructure than for each factory to build its own. (This is the standard reason development corporations exist; the exam tests the name and the full form.)
- **Why is the Ganganagar Sugar Mills the "oldest PSU"?** Because it has a pre-Independence legacy: incorporated in 1945 as "The Bikaner Industrial Corporation Ltd." in the private sector, run privately until 1952, taken over by the state government on **1 July 1956**, renamed "The Ganganagar Sugar Mills Ltd." in 1957 and "Rajasthan State Ganganagar Sugar Mills Ltd." in 1993. The state owns 99.97% of its shares. The official excise department page confirms it is the oldest PSU of the Government of Rajasthan.
- **Why is RSTA in this chapter and in Chapter 3?** Because RSTA (Rajasthan State Testing Agency) is a *new institution* announced in Budget 2026-27 — it belongs to the budget as an announcement (Chapter 3) and to institutions as a body (this chapter). Cross-references are not duplication; they are the same fact seen from two syllabus topics.

### D. HOW DOES IT WORK? — how to answer an institution question

1. **Ask "full form ya kaam?"** — RIICO's full form is the commonest question; its work (industrial areas) is the second.
2. **Ask "PSU ya institution?"** — RSGSM is a PSU (a company the state owns). RIICO is a corporation that *develops* industry. Do not call one the other.
3. **Ask "sabse purana kaun?"** — for PSUs, the answer is Ganganagar Sugar Mills. The "oldest" question is a standard exam angle (compare: oldest sugar mill in the private sector is Mewar Sugar Mills, Bhopal Sagar, 1932 — a *different* question; do not mix them).
4. **If the question is "DIGGI construction"** — the repo has only the PYQ subtopic (2024-P4 Q25). Do not guess what DIGGI is; mark it for verification (see §I).

### B. STORY-BASED EXPLANATION — why this topic is here at all

> **How to use this diagram:** Cover the numbers and say them from the labels: 100 days, 2005, rural households. Then say the one word that matters most — "guarantee".

### B. STORY-BASED EXPLANATION — work as a right, not a favour

Before 2005, rural employment schemes were programmes the government *chose* to run. MGNREGA changed the logic: the **Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act, 2005** made work a **legal guarantee** — a rural household can *demand* 100 days of wage employment in a year, and the state must provide it. The work itself is village work: water conservation, roads, ponds — the same works that double as drought relief (Chapter 6). The Gram Panchayat plans and executes much of it (Chapter 10). That is why MGNREGA sits at the centre of this subject: it is at once an employment scheme, a drought-relief tool, and the Panchayat's main economic job. And it is also why the reported replacement — VB-G RAM G (Chapter 12) — matters so much: if the guarantee changes, the whole chain changes with it.

### C. WHY DOES IT HAPPEN? — why a guarantee, and why 100 days

- **Why a *guarantee* and not a scheme?** Because a scheme can be stopped by a budget cut, but a legal guarantee creates an enforceable right: if work is not given, the household is entitled to an unemployment allowance. The word "Guarantee" in the name is the concept the exam tests.
- **Why 100 days?** The Act fixes 100 days of guaranteed wage employment per rural household per year. It is a statutory number — the exam asks it as a fact (bank `06_ECONOMY_RAJASTHAN-012`, key A). It is not derived from economics; it is a legislative choice, and the honest answer to "why 100 and not 120?" is "because the Act says so".
- **Why is MGNREGA linked to drought?** Because in a scarcity year, demand for MGNREGA work rises sharply — it is the relief mechanism. That is the syllabus's own chain: drought (Ch 6) → demand for work → MGNREGA (this chapter) → PRI implementation (Ch 10).
- **Why the VB-G RAM G caveat?** Because press reports of December 2025 legislation say a new Act (VB-G RAM G) replaces MGNREGA with a 125-day guarantee. The audit could not verify this against the gazette (register §H1), so this file states the *reported* change and flags it — it does not teach 125 days as fact.

### D. HOW DOES IT WORK? — how to answer an MGNREGA question

1. **Ask "kitne din?"** — **100 days** per rural household per year (the Act's number).
2. **Ask "kab se?"** — **2005** (the Act).
3. **Ask "kaun?"** — **rural households** (gramin parivar), demanding work.
4. **Ask "guarantee ya scheme?"** — **guarantee** (a legal right, not a discretionary programme).
5. **Ask "VB-G RAM G se kya sambandh?"** — reported replacement with a 125-day guarantee (Dec 2025 legislation) — **unverified, check the gazette before the exam** (Chapter 12).
