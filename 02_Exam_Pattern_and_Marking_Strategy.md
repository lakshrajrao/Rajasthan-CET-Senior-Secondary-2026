# 02 — Exam Pattern & Negative-Marking Strategy

120 questions · 240 marks · 150 minutes · +2 / −2/3 · E = 0

## The exact maths

Correct = **+2**. Wrong (A–D) = **−2/3 (−0.667)**. Deliberate skip with **E darkened = 0**. **Blank (no circle) = −2/3** — the notification penalises an unmarked question exactly like a wrong answer (p.22, Rule 3: one-third of the question's marks). **More than 10% blanks (>12 of 120) = disqualified** (p.22, Rule 5). That is the single most expensive avoidable mistake in this exam.

Qualifying line: 40% of 240 = **96 marks** (General/OBC/EWS); 35% = **84 marks** (SC/ST, as reported). CET is only an eligibility test — it does not guarantee a post — but you must clear the line to sit the main recruitments. Aim well above it: a safe target is **130–150 marks** (65–75 correct with few wrong), not 96.

## Expected value — when to guess

| Your position | Chance correct (p) | Expected value | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Certain / near-certain | ~1.00 | +2.00 | **Attempt** |
| 2 options eliminated (50:50) | 0.50 | 0.5×2 − 0.5×0.667 = **+0.667** | **Attempt** — clearly positive |
| 1 option eliminated (1 in 3) | 0.33 | 0.33×2 − 0.67×0.667 = **+0.22** | **Attempt** — small positive; attempt if you have any lean |
| No elimination (1 in 4) | 0.25 | 0.25×2 − 0.75×0.667 = **0.00** | **Neutral — mark E**. Guessing adds nothing on average and adds variance |
| No idea at all / confused between all 4 | <0.25 | Negative | **Mark E** |

## Worked examples (120Q paper)

- **Example 1 — strong paper:** 80 correct, 20 wrong, 20 E → 80×2 − 20×0.667 = 160 − 13.3 = **146.7**.
- **Example 2 — same knowledge, blind guessing instead of E:** 80 correct, 40 wrong, 0 E → 160 − 26.7 = **133.3** — 13 marks lost for nothing.
- **Example 3 — weak paper, disciplined:** 55 correct, 15 wrong, 50 E → 110 − 10 = **100** (passes 96). Same knowledge with blind guesses on the 50: expected +0, but a bad-luck run (say 8/50 correct) gives 63×2 − 42×0.667 = 126 − 28 = **98** — still passing, but with far more downside risk.
- **Example 4 — the blank trap (CORRECTED):** 70 correct, 30 wrong, 20 E → 140 − 20 = **120.0**. The same paper with 20 blanks instead of E → 140 − 20 − 20×0.667 = **106.7, and disqualified outright** (20 blanks > 12). E vs blank is a 13.3-mark swing plus disqualification — never blank.

## OMR rules — do not get these wrong

1. **One circle per question, blue ball-point pen only.**
2. **Skip = darken E.** Never leave a question with no circle.
3. **Never darken two circles** for one question.
4. **Use the extra 10 minutes** after the paper to verify OMR circles — check every E, check question numbers against circles, check no question is blank.
5. Keep blanks at **zero** and the 10% disqualification rule can never touch you.

## Time plan — 150 minutes for 120 questions

| Phase | Time | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Pass 1 — certain + easy | 0–70 min | Answer everything you know cold. ~75 sec/Q max. Mark doubtful Qs in the booklet, darken E provisionally on the OMR so nothing is blank |
| Pass 2 — 50:50 / 1-eliminated | 70–120 min | Return to ticked Qs. Eliminate, attempt where EV is positive. Reasoning/Maths Qs that need calculation go here, not Pass 1 |
| Pass 3 — final E sweep | 120–140 min | Every remaining Q: if p < 0.33, darken E firmly. Count Es — they should be your deliberate skips, not accidents |
| OMR verification | 140–150 min + extra 10 min | Question-number alignment, no blanks, no double marks, E circles dark. Do not change a certain answer in the last minutes |

**Attempt discipline in one line: attempt when you can eliminate at least one option; mark E when you cannot — and never, ever leave a circle blank.**
